Imagine exporting your church's member list and always getting the same five columns. I opened a fix for ChurchCRM, an open-source church management system, that lets users choose details like birth date or phone number in a CSV export instead.

I shared it as a pull request, a proposed change the project's team can review. An automated reviewer asked for one small change, and after I made it, it approved the work about 12 hours later. No project maintainer has replied yet, so I am waiting for a human review.

What it taught me:
1. Pick the right problem: I compared several issues against a checklist first
2. Follow the community's rules: I claimed the issue first and kept my change focused
3. Check my own work: I wrote a test and ran my own checks before opening the request

Thank you to the ChurchCRM team and my CodePath AI301 course.
